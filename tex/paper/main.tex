\begin{abstract}
Hierarchical federated learning reduces direct cloud communication, but a fixed training workflow cannot accommodate heterogeneous devices or changing cloud-edge-end conditions.
 This paper studies dynamic collaboration reconfiguration for federated learning. 
 The execution and aggregation path is selected from seven cloud-edge-end collaboration modes according to the current system state.
 A mode first determines the objects to transmit and their communication paths. Privacy protection is then assigned according to the resulting risk.
The noise multiplier is calibrated per client according to its accumulated privacy loss and the number of remaining training rounds, so that the privacy budget is consumed appropriately across the whole training process.
Feasible global profiles are evaluated based on system latency and DP
perturbation cost. They are then filtered by Pareto dominance and selected
using the Chebyshev ideal point criterion. 
Experiments on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 and on Fashion-MNIST with LeNet-5 examine the effects of dynamic collaboration and dynamic privacy calibration under heterogeneous resource and data conditions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Federated learning (FL) enables multiple end devices to train a shared model
without transferring their raw data to a central server~\cite{fedavg}. In AIoT
systems, however, participating devices differ substantially in computation,
memory, communication quality, and local data distribution. Cloud-edge-end
hierarchical federated learning (HFL) introduces edge servers between end devices
and the cloud, reducing communication over long distances and providing additional computation and aggregation capacity~\cite{hfl}.

Two forms of heterogeneity make a fixed cloud-edge-end collaboration procedure
difficult to use across all clients and rounds. First, non-IID data can cause
local updates to drift in different directions and can degrade simple weighted
aggregation~\cite{fedavgm,fedprox}.
Second, system capabilities are heterogeneous. A weak end device may be unable
to handle the same workload or meet the same deadline as a stronger device,
whereas an edge or cloud server can undertake more computation~\cite{wang2019adaptivefl}.
A mode that suits one client in one round may be inefficient or infeasible
for the same client in another round, or for another client.

Existing work on HFL and cloud-edge-end collaboration has covered personalization,
resource allocation, wireless scheduling, heterogeneous models, and split
training~\cite{Personalizedhfl,JSJX202502007,dinh2021wirelessfl,BSBODP,mazloomi2025multiserver,SFETEC}.
However, most of these methods optimize a training workflow under a fixed
hierarchy~\cite{wang2019adaptivefl}. In a cloud-edge-end system, a fixed hierarchy
mismatches the actual condition of each round. A weak device may be assigned a
task it cannot finish, while a strong edge server may sit idle.

The collaboration mode determines what privacy protection is needed. Some updates are visible to the cloud and may reveal client information. Others are aggregated in encrypted form and must stay hidden from the aggregator. No single privacy mechanism fits all modes. We therefore use differential privacy (DP)~\textcolor{red}{[ref needed]}
for updates visible to the cloud, homomorphic encryption (HE)~\textcolor{red}{[ref needed]}
for encrypted aggregation, and keep raw training samples on end devices.

This coupling creates a problem that involves system efficiency, learning
quality, and privacy. Changing the collaboration mode affects where computation is executed, how updates travel, when aggregation happens, and which updates are visible to which party. These choices interact. DP noise directly affects the perturbation cost of protected model
updates, while the remaining privacy budget limits future protected
releases. Figure~\ref{fig:balance} illustrates this interaction.
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\columnwidth]{balance.png}
\caption{Tradeoff among privacy, utility, and efficiency.}
\label{fig:balance}
\end{figure}

This paper studies reconfiguration of cloud-edge-end federated learning at each
selection round under heterogeneous resources and evolving privacy state. For
each client, the system evaluates feasible collaboration and protection profiles
under current conditions rather than using one fixed workflow for all clients.
The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We define an execution space with seven cloud-edge-end collaboration modes,
    including edge only, direct cloud, and edge to cloud training and aggregation
    paths. Each client selects its own mode in each round, and the mode determines
    its actual communication and aggregation topology.
    \item We cast the selection as a multiobjective optimization that balances system latency and a theoretically derived learning error objective under resource and privacy constraints. The learning error objective jointly captures incomplete global fusion and DP-induced update perturbation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}
\subsection{HFL Under Heterogeneous Environments}
HFL introduces edge servers between end devices and the cloud to reduce direct cloud communication pressure through edge pre aggregation~\cite{hfl}.
Resource aware FL studies reduce stragglers and communication overhead by adjusting client selection, local update frequency, pruning, and model compression under limited computation, energy, and bandwidth resources~\cite{phfl,xu2022adaptive,wang2019adaptivefl,dinh2021wirelessfl}.
Personalized HFL further mitigates performance degradation under non-IID data through edge side personalized models, similarity aggregation, and model decoupling~\cite{Personalizedhfl,JSJX202502007}.
These methods show that HFL can improve training from resource scheduling, communication control, and data heterogeneity adaptation.
However, when end device resources differ across computation, memory, and communication capabilities, using the same training flow for all clients can still cause straggler blocking in conventional synchronous aggregation and prevent weak devices from participating effectively.
This paper jointly considers end device computation constraints, memory constraints, communication overhead, and model split positions during collaboration reconfiguration.
Dynamic selection improves flexibility, but reevaluating every round may incur
excessive switching cost. Recent FL studies reduce this cost by keeping the
selected client set unchanged over several rounds~\cite{CHEN2026112204,10757319}.
Our framework introduces a strategy selection period parameter to control
switching cost.

\subsection{Cloud-Edge-End Collaborative Federated Learning}

HFL is a representative form of cloud-edge-end collaborative FL, in which end
devices, edge nodes, and the cloud jointly execute computation, aggregation, and
communication tasks.
As model size and system heterogeneity increase, a fixed scheme where end
devices train the full model and the edge aggregates the updates becomes less
suitable for IoT devices with limited resources.
FedAgg~\cite{BSBODP} uses bridge samples and online distillation to enable
models of different scales at the end, edge, and cloud layers to collaborate.
SFETEC~\cite{SFETEC} introduces split federated learning into Thing Edge Cloud
environments and partitions model computation across layers to reduce the
computation load of end devices.
Cloud-edge-end task offloading studies also combine FL, privacy protection, and
task efficiency~\cite{dong2026privacyoffloading}.
These studies show the value of collaboration among the three layers, but they
usually assume a fixed collaboration structure.
In contrast, this paper studies how each device should select a collaborative
mode under given resource and privacy conditions.

The need to balance reconfiguration gain and reconfiguration cost is also
consistent with edge computing task migration studies. In mobile edge computing,
migration can reduce service delay when users move away from the original edge
server, but it also consumes backhaul transmission and reconfiguration
resources~\cite{ZHANG2019111}. Task migration that accounts for load further
models migration cost as a combination of communication, queuing, and
computation costs, and notes that migration cost can be related to the physical
distance between edge nodes~\cite{ZHU2024303}. These studies suggest that
dynamic cloud-edge-end collaboration should account for both the benefit of
changing execution paths and the overhead caused by such changes.

\subsection{Privacy Preserving HFL}
Model parameters, gradients, and intermediate representations in federated
training may leak information about private data.
Differential privacy (DP), homomorphic encryption (HE), and secure aggregation
are therefore widely used in FL.
For HFL, group local DP provides personalized privacy through sampling,
randomized response, and shuffling~\cite{GLDP}.
Adaptive DP FL improves convergence and model utility through dynamic privacy
budget allocation, adaptive clipping, and client selection~\cite{xu2025adpfl}.
Other HFL studies with privacy protection combine per client differential
privacy, secure aggregation, and HE based on CKKS for data sharing
scenarios~\cite{PPHFL}.
Related fog FL studies combine hierarchical aggregation, personalization, and DP
for IoT intrusion detection~\cite{islam2025pphffl}.
These studies mainly focus on the security guarantee, budget allocation, or
accuracy degradation of a particular privacy mechanism.
The joint modeling of privacy-induced communication overhead, computation
latency, DP perturbation, and cloud-edge-end collaboration reconfiguration
has not been sufficiently studied.

This paper jointly reconfigures the complete federated learning workflow.
Existing categories optimize selected resources, training steps, or privacy
parameters within a prescribed collaboration structure. The proposed
collaboration space makes complete workflows with different execution
locations, aggregation paths, transmitted objects, and privacy mechanisms
comparable under one feasible decision model.
\section{Model Construction}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Collaborative Mode and Transmission Modeling}
\label{sec:link_model}

The proposed system is a cloud-edge-end collaborative federated learning architecture with end devices, edge nodes, and one cloud node. Let \(N\) be the number of end devices. Each admitted client \(i\) can execute its own mode \(h_i^t\) in communication round \(t\). This mode specifies the execution, communication, and aggregation path within the cloud-edge-end federated learning process.

\textbf{Collaboration interfaces.}

interfaces based on where communication occurs across tiers in the training process. Interface I supports split training, where the Local tier exchanges
intermediate information with the Edge or Cloud tier during training.
Interface II submits a model update to the Edge or Cloud for aggregation
after a training stage. Interface III submits an aggregated Edge update to
the Cloud after Edge aggregation.

Following these interfaces, we define seven client level collaboration modes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H=
\{\mathrm{LIE},\mathrm{LIIE},\mathrm{LIC},\mathrm{LIIC},
\mathrm{LIEIIC},\mathrm{LIEIIIC},\mathrm{LIIEIIIC}\}.
\label{eq:seven_modes}
\end{equation}
For example, LIE (\(L\)-I-\(E\)) splits the model between the Local and
Edge tiers for split training. LIEIIC further submits the resulting Edge update to the Cloud through Interface II.
LIEIIIC (\(L\)-I-\(E\)-III-\(C\)) extends LIE with repeated Edge
aggregation stages before forwarding the Edge update to the Cloud.

Given the current model state \(M^t\), split modes execute the corresponding
model blocks across tiers, denoted by \(M_L^{h_i}\), \(M_E^{h_i}\), and
\(M_C^{h_i}\) when present. Local update modes instead complete the training
stage at the end device before submitting a model update to the next
aggregation tier. Raw training samples remain on the end device in every mode.

\subsubsection{Collaborative Link Blocks and Mode Flows}
A collaborative link block represents the basic training unit performed by a
client within one collaboration stage. We consider two types of blocks.
For client \(i\) in round \(t\), let
\begin{equation}
N_i^t
=
E_i^t
\left\lceil
\frac{|\mathcal D_i^t|}{B_i^t}
\right\rceil,
\label{eq:split_interaction_count}
\end{equation}
where \(E_i^t\) is the number of local epochs, \(|\mathcal D_i^t|\) is the
number of local training samples, and \(B_i^t\) is the minibatch size. Thus
\(N_i^t\) is the workload-derived number of optimizer steps and, for a split
mode, the number of interactive split exchanges in one collaboration stage.
A split training block \(S_{Lr}(N_i^t)\), \(r\in\{E,C\}\), performs these
steps jointly between the Local tier and tier \(r\) through split interaction.
A local training block \(T_L(N_i^t)\) performs the same local workload entirely
at the Local tier and produces one model update for subsequent aggregation.
The optimizer workload and the number of network releases are therefore kept
conceptually separate: split modes communicate once per split step, whereas
update-submission modes communicate the resulting update after the local
training block.

A collaborative link flow describes how the training blocks, update
transmissions, and aggregation stages are connected under a collaboration
mode. Let \(A_E\) and \(A_C\) denote aggregation at the Edge and Cloud,
respectively. For hierarchical modes, \(E_B\) denotes the number of repeated
Edge stages before the Edge update is submitted to the Cloud.

With these definitions, the seven mode flows are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{LIE}:~~&
S_{LE}(N_i^t) \rightarrow A_E,\\
\mathrm{LIIE}:~~&
T_L(N_i^t) \xrightarrow{L\to E:\mathrm{upd}} A_E,\\
\mathrm{LIC}:~~&
S_{LC}(N_i^t) \rightarrow A_C,\\
\mathrm{LIIC}:~~&
T_L(N_i^t) \xrightarrow{L\to C:\mathrm{upd}} A_C,\\
\mathrm{LIEIIC}:~~&
S_{LE}(N_i^t)
\xrightarrow{E\to C:\mathrm{upd}} A_C,\\
\mathrm{LIEIIIC}:~~&
\bigl[S_{LE}(N_i^t)\rightarrow A_E\bigr]^{E_B}
\xrightarrow{E\to C:\mathrm{upd}} A_C,\\
\mathrm{LIIEIIIC}:~~&
\bigl[
T_L(N_i^t)
\xrightarrow{L\to E:\mathrm{upd}} A_E
\bigr]^{E_B}
\xrightarrow{E\to C:\mathrm{upd}} A_C .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mode_flows}
\end{equation}

Orange links represent collaborative link block execution, with each block containing the workload-derived \(N_i^t\) training steps.
Green links represent execution at the Edge stage in hierarchical modes. This execution is repeated \(E_B\) times before the resulting Edge update is forwarded to the Cloud.
Black self loops represent the final aggregation at the corresponding aggregation node. Black arrows represent update forwarding and model return paths among end devices, Edge nodes, and the Cloud. These flows determine the structure of the directed payload transmissions \(\mathcal X_i^t(h)\) presented below.

\subsubsection{Mode Induced Transmission and Protection}
\begin{definition}[Transmission set induced by a mode]
For device \(i\), round \(t\), and collaboration mode \(h\), define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal X_i^t(h)
=
\left\{
(u,v,b,n)
\;\middle|\;
(u,v,b)\text{ occurs }n\text{ times under }h
\right\}.
\label{eq:transmission_set}
\end{equation}
Here \(u,v\in\{L,E,C\}\), \(b\in\mathcal B\), and \(n\) is the multiplicity of the directed transmission in one complete mode flow.
The payload set is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal B=
\{\mathrm{emb},\mathrm{logits},\mathrm{grad},
\mathrm{emb\_grad},\mathrm{upd}\}.
\end{equation*}
The five payload types have the following meanings.
\(\mathrm{emb}\) is the intermediate representation produced by the local model block.
\(\mathrm{logits}\) is the prediction output returned by the remote model block.
\(\mathrm{grad}\) is the loss gradient with respect to the returned logits.
\(\mathrm{emb\_grad}\) is the gradient with respect to the transmitted embedding.
\(\mathrm{upd}\) is a model update used for aggregation or model state synchronization.
Thus \(\mathcal X_i^t(h)\) describes the physical communication events induced by mode \(h\).
\end{definition}
The transmission set \(\mathcal X_i^t(h)\) identifies the directed payload transmissions to which the protection requirements are applied. For each such transmission, define the protection map
\[
\pi_i^{t,h}(u,v,b)
\in
\{\mathrm{none},\mathrm{HE},\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{HE{+}DP}\},
\]
where \(\mathrm{none}\) denotes plaintext transmission, while \(\mathrm{HE}\), \(\mathrm{DP}\), and \(\mathrm{HE{+}DP}\) denote homomorphic encryption, differential privacy, and their combination, respectively. The value of \(\pi_i^{t,h}(u,v,b)\) is determined by the protection requirement associated with the corresponding transmission. Thus \(\pi_i^{t,h}\) is induced by the candidate mode \(h\) and its transmission requirements rather than being an independent decision variable.

Among these transmissions, those assigned a mechanism that uses DP generate formal privacy accounting events. These events are defined in the next subsection.

\subsection{Lifetime Privacy Accounting}
\label{sec:privacy_model}

The privacy cost of a collaboration mode depends on the update
transmissions that use DP protection. For candidate mode \(h_i\), we define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D_i^t(h)
=
\bigl\{
&(u,v,b,n)\in\mathcal X_i^t(h)
\;\bigm|\;
b=\mathrm{upd},\\
&\pi_i^{t,h}(u,v,b)
\in\{\mathrm{DP},\mathrm{HE{+}DP}\}
\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dp_exposures}
\end{equation}
Thus, \(\mathcal D_i^t(h)\) contains the DP protected update
transmissions of device \(i\) under mode \(h\).

For each client, we use R\'enyi differential privacy (RDP)
accounting~\cite{mironov2017renyi,wang2019subsampled} to accumulate the
privacy cost of these events across training rounds. Let
\(\mathcal A\) denote the set of R\'enyi orders used by the accountant.
For a Gaussian DP event with noise multiplier \(\sigma\), its RDP cost at
order \(\alpha\in\mathcal A\) is
\begin{equation}
\rho(\alpha,\sigma)
=
\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^2}.
\label{eq:rdp_event_cost}
\end{equation}
A larger noise multiplier therefore results in a smaller RDP cost for the
released update.

Let \(\Phi_i^t(\alpha)\) denote the accumulated RDP cost of device \(i\)
at the beginning of round \(t\). If \(m_i^t\) DP events are released in
round \(t\), with noise multipliers
\(\sigma_{i,1}^t,\ldots,\sigma_{i,m_i^t}^t\), the privacy state is updated
by
\begin{equation}
\Phi_i^{t+1}(\alpha)
=
\Phi_i^t(\alpha)
+
\sum_{k=1}^{m_i^t}
\rho(\alpha,\sigma_{i,k}^t).
\label{eq:rdp_update}
\end{equation}
Hence, privacy loss is accumulated only from DP events that are actually
released.

For a given \(\delta\), the accumulated RDP state is converted to the
corresponding privacy loss as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^t
=
\min_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}
\left[
\Phi_i^t(\alpha)
+
\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}
\right].
\label{eq:rdp_to_dp}
\end{equation}
The privacy requirement over the complete training process is
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_i^t
\le
\bar{\epsilon}.
\label{eq:lifetime_privacy_constraint}
\end{equation}

The required noise level depends on how many DP events a candidate mode
may generate during the remaining training rounds. For candidate mode
\(h\), the number of DP events generated in one round is
\begin{equation}
n_{i,h}^{t,\mathrm{DP}}
=
\sum_{(u,v,b,n)\in\mathcal D_i^t(h)} n.
\label{eq:dp_events_per_round}
\end{equation}
There are \(R-t+1\) rounds remaining, including the current round.
Using the current candidate mode to determine the number of DP events per
round, the number considered for the remaining training process is
\begin{equation}
N_{i,h}^{t,\mathrm{plan}}
=
n_{i,h}^{t,\mathrm{DP}}(R-t+1).
\label{eq:planned_update_events}
\end{equation}

When \(n_{i,h}^{t,\mathrm{DP}}>0\), the noise multiplier
\(\sigma_{i,h_i}^t\) is numerically selected using the current RDP state
\(\{\Phi_i^t(\alpha)\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}\), the planned number of DP
events \(N_{i,h}^{t,\mathrm{plan}}\), and the privacy target
\((\bar{\epsilon},\delta)\). The selected value must keep the accumulated
privacy loss in \eqref{eq:rdp_to_dp} within
\(\bar{\epsilon}\) after accounting for the planned DP events. If no such
value can be found, the candidate mode does not satisfy the privacy
constraint. When \(n_{i,h}^{t,\mathrm{DP}}=0\), the mode generates no DP
event and requires no noise calibration.

For a hierarchical Edge group \(g\), the Edge update is released to the Cloud as a single packet with DP protection. The noise
multiplier used for this packet is derived from the calibrated client
multipliers of the participating devices as
\begin{equation}
\sigma_g^t
=
\max_{i\in\mathcal G_g}
\sigma_{i,h_i}^t .
\label{eq:edge_group_noise_multiplier}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\sigma_g^t\) is derived from the client multipliers in the group.

After a mode is selected and executed, only the DP events generated in
that round are added to \(\Phi_i^t(\alpha)\) according to
\eqref{eq:rdp_update}. The updated privacy state is then used to evaluate
the candidate modes in the next round.
\subsection{Joint Cost and Utility Modeling}
\label{sec:cost_utility_model}
Given the link topology, each mode induces its transmissions and the corresponding protection. Its privacy feasibility is determined by the lifetime accounting in Sec.~\ref{sec:privacy_model}. Each mode is then evaluated by resource feasibility, path latency, and learning error objective.
\subsubsection{End Side Resource Feasibility}

Each collaboration mode must satisfy the computation and memory capacities
of the end device. Let \(W_{i,L}^{\mathrm{comp}}(h_i)\) and
\(M_{i,L}^{\mathrm{mem}}(h_i)\) denote the profiled computation and memory
requirements of device \(i\) under mode \(h_i\), respectively, and let
\(Q_{\mathrm{comp},i}\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{mem},i}\) denote the corresponding
available resource capacities. The resource-feasible mode set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{H}_i^{\mathrm{res}}
=
\left\{
h_i\in\mathcal{H}
\;\middle|\;
W_{i,L}^{\mathrm{comp}}(h_i)\le Q_{\mathrm{comp},i},
\;
M_{i,L}^{\mathrm{mem}}(h_i)\le Q_{\mathrm{mem},i}
\right\}.
\label{eq:resource_feasible}
\end{equation}
We further distinguish the full-local/update family
\(\mathcal H^{\mathrm{FL}}=\{\mathrm{LIIE},\mathrm{LIIC},\mathrm{LIIEIIIC}\}\)
and the split/offloading family
\(\mathcal H^{\mathrm{SP}}=\{\mathrm{LIE},\mathrm{LIC},\mathrm{LIEIIC},\mathrm{LIEIIIC}\}\).
DynFL follows an FL-first, split-on-demand admissibility rule. If at least
one full-local mode is resource feasible, dynamic mode selection is restricted
to the feasible full-local family. Split modes are activated when no full-local
mode satisfies the end-side computation and memory constraints. Fixed baselines
are evaluated independently of this dynamic admissibility rule. Accordingly,
the dynamic admissible set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_i^{\mathrm{adm}}
=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal H_i^{\mathrm{res}}\cap\mathcal H^{\mathrm{FL}},
&\text{if }\mathcal H_i^{\mathrm{res}}\cap\mathcal H^{\mathrm{FL}}\neq\varnothing,\\
\mathcal H_i^{\mathrm{res}},&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:fl_first_admissible}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Path and Round Latency on a Common Timeline}

We construct a common timeline for the selected collaboration modes to determine their aggregation arrivals and the resulting round latency.

\textbf{Transmission latency.}
For a transmission of payload \(b\) from tier \(u\) to tier \(v\), let \(S_{u,v,b}^t(h_i)\) denote its raw payload size. Let \(B_{i,u,v}^t\) denote the transmission rate of device \(i\) over \(u\!\to\!v\) in round \(t\), and let \(D_{u,v}^{0}\) denote the fixed base delay. Under the protection mechanism \(\pi_i^{t,h_i}(u,v,b)\) defined above, the transmission latency is
\begin{equation}
T_{u,v,b}^{\mathrm{link},t}(h_i)
=
\frac{
\alpha\!\left(\pi_i^{t,h_i}(u,v,b)\right)
S_{u,v,b}^t(h_i)}
{B_{i,u,v}^t}
+
D_{u,v}^{0}
+
T_{u,v,b}^{\mathrm{priv},t}
\!\left(\pi_i^{t,h_i}(u,v,b)\right),
\label{eq:comm_latency}
\end{equation}
where \(\alpha(\cdot)\) is the payload expansion factor caused by the protection mechanism and \(T_{u,v,b}^{\mathrm{priv},t}(\cdot)\) is the corresponding protection processing latency. For an unprotected transmission, \(\alpha(\mathrm{none})=1\) and \(T_{u,v,b}^{\mathrm{priv},t}(\mathrm{none})=0\).

\textbf{Execution latency.}
Let \(\tau_{i,L}^t(h_i)\) and \(\tau_{i,r}^t(h_i)\), \(r\in\{E,C\}\), denote
the Local and remote-tier execution times within one training step under mode
\(h_i\). For split training, the \(N_i^t\) workload-derived steps may be
pipelined across the two participating tiers, giving
\begin{equation}
T_i^{\mathrm{stage},t}(h_i)
=
N_i^t\max\!\left\{
\tau_{i,L}^t(h_i),\tau_{i,r}^t(h_i)
\right\}
+
\min\!\left\{
\tau_{i,L}^t(h_i),\tau_{i,r}^t(h_i)
\right\},\qquad r\in\{E,C\}.
\label{eq:execution_latency}
\end{equation}
This expression is the ideal two-stage pipeline bound; all directed split
payloads in \(\mathcal X_i^t(h)\) retain multiplicity \(N_i^t\), so base link
delay and transmission time are incurred at every interactive step. For local
training, \(T_i^{\mathrm{stage},t}(h_i)=N_i^t\tau_{i,L}^t(h_i)\), while the
resulting update is submitted once after the local training block. Hierarchical
modes repeat the corresponding Edge stage \(E_B\) times.

\textbf{Reconfiguration latency.}
Changing the collaboration mode may require model reconfiguration across tiers. The resulting delay is
\begin{equation}
T_i^{\mathrm{sw},t}
=
\eta_h \mathbb{I}(h_i^t\neq h_i^{t-1})
+
\eta_p
\frac{
\|\mathbf{p}(h_i^t)-\mathbf{p}(h_i^{t-1})\|_1
}{2},
\label{eq:switching_cost}
\end{equation}
where \(\eta_h\) and \(\eta_p\) are the coefficients for the mode change cost and the placement change cost, respectively~\cite{CHEN2026112204,li2026latency}. The normalized model placement is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}(h_i^t)
=
\frac{
\left[
\lvert M_L^{h_i}\rvert,
\lvert M_E^{h_i}\rvert,
\lvert M_C^{h_i}\rvert
\right]
}{
\lvert M_L^{h_i}\rvert+
\lvert M_E^{h_i}\rvert+
\lvert M_C^{h_i}\rvert
}.
\label{eq:model_placement_vector}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Path arrival and aggregation.}
For a given candidate mode \(h_i\), its mode flow specifies the execution stages and payload transmissions leading to the corresponding aggregation point. The execution and transmission latencies defined above, together with the reconfiguration delay \(T_i^{\mathrm{sw},t}\), are advanced in their order of occurrence along the mode flow. We use \(a_{i,E}^t\) and \(a_{i,C}^t\) to denote the arrival times of the update from device \(i\) at the Edge and Cloud, respectively, whenever the corresponding aggregation point is present in the selected mode.

Once a candidate mode profile \(\mathbf h^t=(h_1^t,\ldots,h_N^t)\) is specified, these arrivals are placed on the common timeline. For an Edge aggregation group \(g\), let \(\mathcal G_g\) denote the devices participating in this group. Since aggregation waits for all required updates, the Edge aggregation completion time is
\begin{equation}
f_{g,E}^t
=
\max_{i\in\mathcal G_g} a_{i,E}^t
+
T_{g,E}^{\mathrm{agg},t},
\label{eq:edge_agg_finish}
\end{equation}
where \(T_{g,E}^{\mathrm{agg},t}\) is the Edge aggregation duration. Updates arriving earlier remain buffered until aggregation starts.

If the selected mode continues to the Cloud, the Edge aggregation result is transmitted to the Cloud after \(f_{g,E}^t\). Let \(a_{g,C}^t\) denote the arrival time of this aggregated update at the Cloud, computed by adding the Edge to Cloud transmission latency to \(f_{g,E}^t\). Modes that send updates directly to the Cloud independently produce the client arrivals \(a_{i,C}^t\). The Cloud aggregation therefore waits for the required arrivals from both sources, and its completion time is
\begin{equation}
f_C^t
=
\max\left\{
\max_i a_{i,C}^t,\,
\max_g a_{g,C}^t
\right\}
+
T_C^{\mathrm{agg},t},
\label{eq:cloud_agg_finish}
\end{equation}
where the two maxima are taken over the direct Cloud clients and the Edge
groups participating in the current Cloud aggregation, respectively.

Return transmissions, when required by the selected mode, further advance
the corresponding aggregation completion times. As illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:time}, the execution, transmission, aggregation, and return
events of the selected mode profile are placed on the common timeline.
The latest completion time on this timeline is denoted by
\(T_{\mathrm{sys}}(\mathbf h^t)\), which represents the system round
latency.

\subsubsection{Learning Error and DP Perturbation Cost}
\label{sec:learning_cost}

The selector evaluates a candidate mode profile using quantities available at selection time. When updates are aggregated across distributed tiers, two distinct distortion sources affect global convergence: (i) deterministic global fusion bias induced by partial Cloud coverage, and (ii) stochastic perturbation variance injected for differential privacy.

For a client update released directly to the Cloud, let $C_u$ be the update clipping norm. Under replacement adjacency, the sensitivity bound is $\Delta_u=2C_u$, where the factor two accounts for the maximum change between two clipped updates. For an update vector of dimension $d_{\mathrm{upd}}$, the Gaussian perturbation associated with client $i$ under mode $h_i^t$ has expected squared magnitude $d_{\mathrm{upd}}(\sigma_{i,h_i}^t\Delta_u)^2$, where $\sigma_{i,h_i}^t$ is the noise multiplier calibrated from the lifetime privacy state.

For a hierarchical Edge group $g$, the participating client updates are first clipped and aggregated at the Edge. The aggregation weight of client $i$ within Edge group $g$ is $w_{i\mid g}=n_i/(\sum_{j\in\mathcal G_g}n_j)$. The sensitivity of the resulting Edge update is bounded by the largest client contribution in the group, $\Delta_g^t=2C_u\max_{i\in\mathcal G_g}w_{i\mid g}$. This Edge update is then released to the Cloud as a single packet with DP protection, and its noise multiplier is $\sigma_g^t$, as defined in \eqref{eq:edge_group_noise_multiplier}.

For a complete mode profile $\mathbf h^t=(h_1^t,\ldots,h_N^t)$, let $N_C^t(\mathbf h^t)$ denote the total number of represented training samples participating in the current Cloud aggregation. The Cloud aggregation weight of a directly released client update is $n_i/N_C^t(\mathbf h^t)$, and the corresponding weight of an Edge update from group $g$ is $\sum_{i\in\mathcal G_g}n_i/N_C^t(\mathbf h^t)$. The DP perturbation cost of a complete mode profile is then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t)
=
d_{\mathrm{upd}}
\Bigg[
&
\sum_{\text{direct }i\to C}
\left(
\frac{n_i}
{N_C^t(\mathbf h^t)}
\sigma_{i,h_i}^t
\Delta_u
\right)^2
\\
&+
\sum_{\text{Edge group }g\to C}
\left(
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal G_g} n_i}
{N_C^t(\mathbf h^t)}
\sigma_g^t
\Delta_g^t
\right)^2
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dp_perturbation_cost}
\end{equation}

In addition to privacy noise, candidate modes that terminate at the Edge without Cloud forwarding omit client contributions from the global Cloud state, introducing fusion bias. Let $N_{\mathrm{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^N n_i$ denote the total training sample mass across all clients. The sample mass ratio represented at the Cloud under profile $\mathbf h^t$ is
\begin{equation}
r_C^t(\mathbf h^t) = \frac{N_C^t(\mathbf h^t)}{N_{\mathrm{total}}}.
\label{eq:cloud_mass_ratio}
\end{equation}
When $r_C^t(\mathbf h^t) < 1$, the global descent trajectory deviates from full-participation federated aggregation. As formally bounded in Proposition~\ref{prop:learning_bound} (with detailed proof in Appendix), under $\beta$-smooth global loss and step size $\eta \le 1/\beta$, the one-round expected optimization loss increment is upper-bounded by an affine combination of the worst-case fusion deviation and DP variance. By leveraging the step-size condition $\beta \le 1/\eta$, both terms can be bounded with a common coefficient $\frac{1}{2\eta}$. Dropping this shared scaling factor and terms independent of $\mathbf h^t$, we define the formal learning objective evaluated at selection time as
\begin{equation}
\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)
=
4C_u^2\left(1 - r_C^t(\mathbf h^t)\right)^2 + J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t).
\label{eq:j_learn_objective}
\end{equation}
For the boundary case of Edge-only mode profiles where $r_C^t(\mathbf h^t) = 0$, no Cloud aggregation occurs. To prevent division by zero in \eqref{eq:dp_perturbation_cost}, we explicitly define $J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t) = 0$ for such profiles, yielding a maximum fusion penalty of $\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t) = 4C_u^2$. This explicitly ensures that abandoning global fusion is never evaluated as a zero-cost solution.
Here, the weighting constants $\frac{4C_u^2}{\eta}$ and $\beta$ emerge intrinsically from the optimization descent lemma rather than empirical tuning, naturally capturing the fundamental trade-off between global information coverage and privacy distortion.
\subsection{Global Multiobjective Collaborative Optimization}
\label{sec:global_optimization}

For each client, the decision variable is the collaboration mode
\(h_i^t\in\mathcal F_i^t\), where
\(\mathcal F_i^t\subseteq\mathcal H_i^{\mathrm{res}}\subseteq\mathcal H\)
contains the modes that satisfy the end device resource constraints in
\eqref{eq:resource_feasible}, the mechanism requirements of each exposure,
and the lifetime privacy constraint. For each evaluated mode, \(\mathcal X_i^t(h_i)\) is induced first. The exposure requirements then determine the protection map \(\pi_i^{t,h_i}\), and the current privacy ledger determines the noise multiplier \(\sigma_{i,h_i}^t\) when a protected update needs DP. Thus \(\pi_i^{t,h_i}\) and \(\sigma_{i,h_i}^t\) follow from the candidate mode.

A complete mixed mode profile is \(\mathbf h^t=(h_1^t,\ldots,h_N^t)\). The flow evaluator applies the shared Edge and Cloud aggregation and admission rules to a complete profile, so the system feasible set is a subset of the Cartesian product,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t
\subseteq
\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal F_i^t.
\label{eq:global_feasible_set}
\end{equation}
An execution that is not admitted is reported as \textsc{skip}.

For every feasible profile, the flow model produces the round duration $T_{\mathrm{sys}}(\mathbf h^t)$, while the formal learning objective $\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)$ is evaluated through \eqref{eq:j_learn_objective}. The selector therefore minimizes the following two objective functions:
\begin{equation}
\underset{\mathbf h^t\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t}
{\operatorname{minimize}}
\quad
\mathbf f(\mathbf h^t)
=
\begin{bmatrix}
T_{\mathrm{sys}}(\mathbf h^t)\\
\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:global_multiobjective}
\end{equation}

Latency and the learning error are the two objectives in the current implementation. HE and communication processing contribute to the path latency, while partial Cloud participation and DP calibration jointly determine the expected learning error bound.

The nondominated decision set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_{\mathrm P}^t
=
\left\{
\mathbf h^t\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t
\;\middle|\;
\nexists\,\mathbf h^{\prime t}\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t
\text{ such that }
\mathbf h^{\prime t}\prec\mathbf h^t
\right\},
\label{eq:pareto_front}
\end{equation}
where \(\prec\) denotes Pareto dominance with respect to the two objectives in \eqref{eq:global_multiobjective}. In the bounded solver, the maintained nondominated archive approximates this set.

The Tchebycheff stage normalizes the two objectives over the current nondominated archive. Let $T_{\min}^t,T_{\max}^t$ and $J_{\min}^t,J_{\max}^t$ denote the minimum and maximum values of the latency and learning error objectives observed in that archive. These values define the normalization range within the current archive. The normalized objectives are
\begin{equation}
\bar T(\mathbf h^t)
=
\frac{
T_{\mathrm{sys}}(\mathbf h^t)-T_{\min}^t
}{
T_{\max}^t-T_{\min}^t+\gamma_n
},
\qquad
\bar J(\mathbf h^t)
=
\frac{
\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)-J_{\min}^t
}{
J_{\max}^t-J_{\min}^t+\gamma_n
}.
\label{eq:pareto_normalization}
\end{equation}

The normalized ideal point is the origin. The selected profile minimizes the largest normalized deviation,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
D_\infty(\mathbf h^t)
&=
\max\left\{
\left|\bar T(\mathbf h^t)\right|,
\left|\bar J(\mathbf h^t)\right|
\right\},
\\
\mathbf h^{t\star}
&\in
\arg\min_{\mathbf h^t\in\mathcal H_{\mathrm P}^t}
D_\infty(\mathbf h^t).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:selection}
\end{equation}

The implementation resolves equal distances deterministically using system latency, then \(\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)\), followed by a stable profile identity. Real test accuracy is reserved for experimental evaluation.
\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

The proposed selection procedure operates at strategy update points. At
rounds that are not update points, the previously selected profile is reused
when \(\mathbf h^{t-1}\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t\).

\begin{breakablealgorithm}
\caption{Bounded Pareto Profile Selection with Exposure Specific Protection}
\label{alg:selection}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\REQUIRE
\(N,\{\mathcal H_i^{\mathrm{res}}\}_{i=1}^{N},
\{\Phi_i^t(\alpha)\}_{i=1}^{N},
(\bar\epsilon,\delta)\), system state, \(S_P\),
\(K_{\mathrm P},K_{\mathrm B},I_{\max},\mathbf h^{t-1}\)

\ENSURE
\(\mathbf h^{t\star}\), or \textsc{skip}

\STATE \textbf{Reuse}

\IF{\(t\notin S_P\) and \(\mathbf h^{t-1}\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t\)}
    \STATE Execute \(\mathbf h^{t-1}\) with its protection mechanisms
    \STATE Update \(\Phi_i^{t+1}(\alpha)\) using \eqref{eq:rdp_update} for
    executed and released DP events only
    \RETURN \(\mathbf h^{t-1}\)
\ENDIF

\STATE \textbf{Candidate Construction}

\FOR{\(i=1,\ldots,N\)}
    \STATE \(\mathcal F_i^t\leftarrow\emptyset\)
    \FOR{each \(h_i\in\mathcal H_i^{\mathrm{res}}\)}
        \STATE Construct \(\mathcal X_i^t(h_i)\) and derive
        \(\pi_i^{t,h_i}\), \(\mathcal D_i^t(h_i)\) by
        \eqref{eq:transmission_set}--\eqref{eq:dp_exposures}
        \STATE Compute \(n_{i,h_i}^{t,\mathrm{DP}}\) and
        \(N_{i,h_i}^{t,\mathrm{plan}}\) by
        \eqref{eq:dp_events_per_round}--\eqref{eq:planned_update_events}
        \IF{\(n_{i,h_i}^{t,\mathrm{DP}}>0\)}
            \STATE Calibrate \(\sigma_{i,h_i}^t\) under
            \((\bar\epsilon,\delta)\)
            \IF{no feasible \(\sigma_{i,h_i}^t\) exists}
                \STATE \textbf{continue}
            \ENDIF
        \ENDIF
        \STATE Add \(h_i\) to \(\mathcal F_i^t\)
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR

\STATE \textbf{Pareto Search}

\STATE Construct
\(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal F_i^t\)
using the shared flow, buffer, aggregation, and admission constraints
\STATE Generate a bounded initial candidate set from
\(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t\)

\IF{\(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t=\emptyset\)}
    \RETURN \textsc{skip}
\ENDIF

\STATE Evaluate the initial candidate profiles using
\(T_{\mathrm{sys}}(\mathbf h^t)\) and \(\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)\) via \eqref{eq:j_learn_objective}

\STATE Initialize archive \(\mathcal A\) with the nondominated initial
profiles and queue \(\mathcal B\)

\FOR{\(r=1,\ldots,I_{\max}\)}
    \STATE Generate unvisited single client replacements from
    \(\mathcal A\cup\mathcal B\)
    \STATE Retain only profiles in \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sys}}^t\)
    \STATE Evaluate \(T_{\mathrm{sys}}(\mathbf h^t)\) and
    \(\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)\) via \eqref{eq:j_learn_objective} for the retained profiles
    \STATE Update \(\mathcal A\), keeping at most \(K_{\mathrm P}\)
    nondominated profiles
    \STATE Update \(\mathcal B\), keeping at most \(K_{\mathrm B}\)
    exploratory profiles
    \IF{neither \(\mathcal A\) nor \(\mathcal B\) is updated}
        \STATE \textbf{break}
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR

\STATE Set \(\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm P}^t\leftarrow\mathcal A\)

\STATE \textbf{Profile Selection}

\STATE Normalize the two objectives over
\(\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm P}^t\) by
\eqref{eq:pareto_normalization}

\STATE Select
\[
\mathbf h^{t\star}\in
\arg\min_{\mathbf h^t\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm P}^t}
D_\infty(\mathbf h^t)
\]
with the specified deterministic tie breaking rule

\STATE \textbf{Execution and Accounting}

\STATE Execute \(\mathbf h^{t\star}\) and its protection mechanisms

\STATE Update \(\Phi_i^{t+1}(\alpha)\) using \eqref{eq:rdp_update} for
executed and released DP events only

\RETURN \(\mathbf h^{t\star}\)

\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

R_{i,u,v}^{t}=\bar R_{u,v}[1+0.2\sin(t/3)]\zeta_{i,u,v}^{t},
\qquad \zeta_{i,u,v}^{t}\sim\mathcal U(0.75,1.25).
\label{eq:experiment_link_rate}
\end{equation}
The base rates are 5 MB/s for end to edge links, 2.2 MB/s for end to cloud
links, and 8 MB/s for edge to cloud links. Reverse links use the same base
rate. These values define a reproducible synthetic network scenario rather
than a physical network measurement.

Privacy execution follows the mode-specific exposure contract in
Section~\ref{sec:privacy_model}. A mode first determines its transmitted objects
and links. Under the current paper profile, a Cloud-facing model-update exposure
requires both client-level DP calibration and HE-protected aggregation, whereas
edge-only execution does not acquire an artificial Cloud release. For a dynamic
privacy candidate, the update multiplier is the minimum value feasible under the
client's current ledger and the protected update events planned over the remaining
training horizon. Consequently there is no single noise multiplier shared by all
modes and rounds. The CIFAR 10 classifier update is clipped at $C_u=0.25$ before
the applicable DP mechanism. When a selected candidate uses HE, the formal
configuration requires real SEAL execution; DP and HE retain their distinct
privacy-loss and confidentiality roles. Only DP events that are actually executed
and released are charged to the corresponding client ledger.

All four formal policies use the same data partition, optimizer, training work,
resource process, and flow model. The configured main-comparison seeds are 40,
42, and 44. Controlled studies should use the same seeds unless a study explicitly
states otherwise, and results from older execution revisions are not combined
with the current formal comparison.

Every run stores the complete configuration, runtime environment, exact client
partition, generated system profiles, model parameter partition, raw client
decisions, link events, and round metrics in its output directory.

The system time used for efficiency comparisons is the modeled cloud-edge-end
execution time plus the measured mode selection time. It includes the decision
cost while excluding the sequential host overhead caused by emulating many
clients on one machine. Host wall time is recorded separately as an
implementation measurement and is not used as deployment latency.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented a dynamic cloud-edge-end collaboration reconfiguration
framework for federated learning under resource and privacy constraints. The
unified collaboration space represents execution placement, aggregation
topology, transmitted objects, and return paths as the dynamic decision, while a
mode-specific exposure contract determines where DP and HE are required. The
joint model evaluates latency, communication, candidate-specific DP distortion,
encrypted aggregation overhead, and estimated convergence error cost. The constrained multiobjective formulation is solved by bounded Pareto
search and ideal point Tchebycheff selection.

The current framework uses profiled computation and link rates in its logical
latency model, while the training process and CKKS aggregation are executed on
one host. Future work will combine online profiling with physical
cloud-edge-end deployment and will further reduce reconfiguration overhead
through parallel profile evaluation, distributed search, and warm starts.

\section{Derivation of the Expected Learning Objective Bound}
\label{app:convergence_proof}

This appendix provides the formal proof for the learning objective bound formulated in Sec.~\ref{sec:learning_cost}.

\subsection{System Model and Update Decomposition}
Let $F(w)$ denote the global objective function. We assume that $F$ is $L$-smooth (denoted by $\beta$ in the main text to prevent notation conflict with the Local tier $L$), satisfying
\begin{equation}
F(y) \le F(x) + \langle \nabla F(x), y - x \rangle + \frac{\beta}{2}\|y - x\|_2^2, \quad \forall x, y.
\label{eq:app_smoothness}
\end{equation}

In round $t$, let $\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}^t = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{n_i}{N_{\mathrm{total}}} u_i^t$ denote the ideal, unperturbed full-fusion model update with $\|u_i^t\|_2 \le C_u$. For a given collaboration profile $\mathbf h^t$, let $\mathcal C^t \subseteq \{1,\dots,N\}$ denote the subset of clients whose updates participate in Cloud aggregation (either directly or aggregated via Edge groups). The noise-free profile update received at the Cloud is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{prof}}^t = \sum_{i \in \mathcal C^t} \frac{n_i}{N_C^t(\mathbf h^t)} u_i^t,
\end{equation}
where $N_C^t(\mathbf h^t) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal C^t} n_i$.
The privacy mechanism injects a zero-mean perturbation $z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t(\mathbf h^t)$ with variance $\mathbb E_t[\|z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t(\mathbf h^t)\|_2^2] = J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t)$, yielding the executed global model update:
\begin{equation}
w^{t+1} - w^t = \Delta_{\mathrm{prof}}^t + z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t(\mathbf h^t).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Lemma 1: Bounding Fusion Deviation}
\begin{lemma}
Let $r_C^t(\mathbf h^t) = N_C^t(\mathbf h^t) / N_{\mathrm{total}} \in (0, 1]$ denote the sample mass ratio represented at the Cloud. The squared norm of the fusion deviation $e_{\mathrm{fus}}^t = \Delta_{\mathrm{prof}}^t - \Delta_{\mathrm{full}}^t$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
B_{\mathrm{fus}}^t(\mathbf h^t) := \|e_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2^2 \le 4C_u^2\left(1 - r_C^t(\mathbf h^t)\right)^2.
\label{eq:app_lemma_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\bar u_C^t = \sum_{i \in \mathcal C^t} \frac{n_i}{N_C^t} u_i^t$ and $\bar u_{\bar C}^t = \sum_{i \notin \mathcal C^t} \frac{n_i}{N_{\mathrm{total}} - N_C^t} u_i^t$ be the convex combinations of updates inside and outside the Cloud-participating set, respectively. Then the full update decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}^t = r_C^t \bar u_C^t + (1 - r_C^t)\bar u_{\bar C}^t.
\end{equation}
Since $\Delta_{\mathrm{prof}}^t = \bar u_C^t$, the discrepancy is
\begin{equation}
e_{\mathrm{fus}}^t = \bar u_C^t - \left(r_C^t \bar u_C^t + (1 - r_C^t)\bar u_{\bar C}^t\right) = (1 - r_C^t)(\bar u_C^t - \bar u_{\bar C}^t).
\end{equation}
Applying the triangle inequality under the clipping bound $\|u_i^t\|_2 \le C_u$:
\begin{equation}
\|e_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2 \le (1 - r_C^t)\left(\|\bar u_C^t\|_2 + \|\bar u_{\bar C}^t\|_2\right) \le 2C_u(1 - r_C^t).
\end{equation}
Squaring both sides yields \eqref{eq:app_lemma_bound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proposition 1: One-Round Expected Loss Bound}
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:learning_bound}
Assume the unperturbed full-fusion update provides the true gradient direction, such that $g^t = -\frac{1}{\eta}\Delta_{\mathrm{full}}^t = \nabla F(w^t)$. Under step size $\eta \le 1/\beta$, identifying $b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t = -\frac{1}{\eta}e_{\mathrm{fus}}^t$, the conditional expected loss satisfies
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_t[F(w^{t+1})] \le F(w^t) - \frac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla F(w^t)\|_2^2 + \frac{1}{2\eta}\left[ B_{\mathrm{fus}}^t(\mathbf h^t) + J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t) \right].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $w^{t+1} - w^t = -\eta(g^t + b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t) + z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t$ into \eqref{eq:app_smoothness}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F(w^{t+1}) \le F(w^t) &+ \langle \nabla F(w^t), -\eta(g^t + b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t) + z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t \rangle \\
&+ \frac{\beta}{2}\left\|-\eta(g^t + b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t) + z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t\right\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Taking conditional expectation $\mathbb E_t[\cdot]$ given $w^t$, since the perturbation is zero-mean ($\mathbb E_t[z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t] = 0$) and independent of the current gradients, the inner product with $z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t$ vanishes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_t[F(w^{t+1})] \le F(w^t) &- \eta \langle g^t, g^t + b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t \rangle \\
&+ \frac{\beta\eta^2}{2}\|g^t + b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2^2 + \frac{\beta}{2}\mathbb E_t[\|z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t\|_2^2].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Using the condition $\eta \le 1/\beta \implies \frac{\beta\eta^2}{2} \le \frac{\eta}{2}$, we bound the quadratic term:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&-\eta \langle g^t, g^t + b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t \rangle + \frac{\beta\eta^2}{2}\|g^t + b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2^2 \\
&\le -\eta \|g^t\|_2^2 - \eta \langle g^t, b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t \rangle \\
&\quad + \frac{\eta}{2}\left(\|g^t\|_2^2 + 2\langle g^t, b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t \rangle + \|b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2^2\right) \\
&= - \frac{\eta}{2}\|g^t\|_2^2 + \frac{\eta}{2}\|b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Notice that the cross term $\eta \langle g^t, b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t \rangle$ cancels out identically.
Substituting $\|b_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{\eta^2}\|e_{\mathrm{fus}}^t\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{\eta^2}B_{\mathrm{fus}}^t$ and $\mathbb E_t[\|z_{\mathrm{DP}}^t\|_2^2] = J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t)$ gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_t[F(w^{t+1})] \le F(w^t) - \frac{\eta}{2}\|g^t\|_2^2 + \frac{1}{2\eta}B_{\mathrm{fus}}^t(\mathbf h^t) + \frac{\beta}{2}J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t).
\end{equation}
Using the step-size assumption $\eta \le 1/\beta \implies \beta \le 1/\eta$, we can further upper-bound the DP term coefficient as $\frac{\beta}{2} \le \frac{1}{2\eta}$. Applying Lemma 1 to bound $B_{\mathrm{fus}}^t(\mathbf h^t) \le 4C_u^2(1 - r_C^t(\mathbf h^t))^2$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_t[F(w^{t+1})] \le F(w^t) - \frac{\eta}{2}\|g^t\|_2^2 + \frac{1}{2\eta}\left[ 4C_u^2(1 - r_C^t(\mathbf h^t))^2 + J_{\mathrm{DP}}(\mathbf h^t) \right].
\end{equation}
Dropping the terms independent of $\mathbf h^t$ and factoring out the common positive scaling constant $\frac{1}{2\eta}$, we establish the surrogate objective $\widehat J_{\mathrm{learn}}^t(\mathbf h^t)$ in \eqref{eq:j_learn_objective}.
\end{proof}
